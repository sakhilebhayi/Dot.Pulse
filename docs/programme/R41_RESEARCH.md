# Dot.Pulse market research: business social platforms, directories, marketplaces and job portals

Research date: 7 November 2026. Method: web search across news, reviews, forums, regulator and law-firm commentary. Evidence is paraphrased. Where a claim rests on a vendor blog, a single review or a petition, it is flagged. This is product research, not legal advice; POPIA points should be confirmed with counsel before they become design rules.

Evidence quality in one line: strong for LinkedIn, Facebook Marketplace, Gumtree SA, ghost jobs, Thumbtack, Bark, Upwork, Glassdoor and Pinterest; moderate for Nextdoor, Alibaba, Fiverr and WhatsApp commerce in South Africa; thin for X, Bumble Bizz, Contra, Wellfound, Jobberman, PNet, CareerJunction and Brabys-style SA directories (I found guidance and scam warnings, not complaint data).

## 2. The ten most repeated frustrations

1. **Silence after effort.** Applicants meet ghost jobs and unanswered applications on Indeed and LinkedIn Easy Apply; Wellfound users describe the same. A LinkedIn survey found 37 percent of job seekers heard back less as they applied more. Sellers on Facebook Marketplace describe being ghosted after a one-line enquiry. Both sides are ignored by the other.
2. **Generic first contact.** "Is this still available?" on Marketplace and Gumtree, copy-paste InMail on LinkedIn and unspecific Bark leads all start a conversation with no information in it.
3. **Pay to be seen.** Thumbtack and Bark charge per lead, Upwork charges Connects to bid, Nextdoor and Instagram push sponsored reach, directories sell listings. Small operators say paying does not reliably produce work.
4. **The same opportunity sold to many.** Bark routes a lead to up to five pros and Thumbtack reviewers say the same job goes to several contractors (Thumbtack disputes this framing). The effect is a speed race and low win rates.
5. **Scams that exploit the platform's own trust.** Fake proof of payment and phishing on Gumtree and Marketplace, fake learnerships on social media aimed at SA graduates, fake suppliers on Alibaba, fake recruiters on LinkedIn.
6. **Pressure to leave the platform.** Scammers move buyers to WhatsApp or direct bank transfer (Gumtree, Marketplace, Alibaba), which strips whatever protection existed. Gumtree itself tells users to stay in-platform.
7. **Feed noise and algorithmic opacity.** LinkedIn feed filled with unrelated pitches and engagement bait; Instagram organic reach described by small owners as dead; Pinterest flooded with AI imagery and ads. Users cannot tell why they see what they see.
8. **Reviews and ratings that cannot be trusted.** Fiverr buyers and sellers report purchased and pressured reviews (percentage claims in blogs contradict each other, so none are relied on here). Glassdoor's credibility rests on anonymity, which was shaken in 2024.
9. **Weak or one-sided dispute handling.** Alibaba, Upwork and Fiverr buyers and sellers each describe the platform siding with the other party or taking no action. Evidence is anecdotal but consistent.
10. **Identity pressure and lost control.** Glassdoor adding real names to profiles caused account deletions; Nextdoor bans arrive with little explanation; Facebook forces a personal identity onto a sales activity. People want to decide who sees them and when.

## 3. Where experiences turn repetitive, transactional, overwhelming or disconnected

**Repetitive.** Every marketplace asks the same three questions in the same order: available? last price? where? A seller on Marketplace answers them dozens of times per listing. Job seekers retype the same CV into each portal. Freelancers on Upwork write fresh proposals for jobs that dozens of others are also pitching. The loop is repeated labour with no memory of previous answers.

**Transactional.** Thumbtack and Bark reduce a person to a purchased lead. Alibaba reduces a supplier to a response rate and a badge. LinkedIn turns a connection into a pitch target. The relationship only begins after the money or the data changes hands, and ends when the job closes. Nothing rewards a user for being helpful when no deal is on the table. Clubhouse was one of the few attempts at unforced conversation and it failed on discovery and moderation, not on the idea.

**Overwhelming.** Feeds on LinkedIn, Instagram, Pinterest and Facebook never end, and content volume grows with AI-generated filler. Recruiters and sellers face inbox floods; job seekers face hundreds of near-identical listings. Overwhelm is partly deliberate: more content means more time on site, which sells more advertising.

**Disconnected.** The South African reality is a sharp example. About three quarters of surveyed township SMEs reach customers over WhatsApp, which has no search or discovery, while discovery happens on Facebook, Gumtree or word of mouth, and the sale finishes in cash. Each step lives in a different app. Job seeking is the same: Facebook posts, PNet, CareerJunction, a WhatsApp group and a friend's referral, none connected. Even within one product, the profile, the message and the transaction do not share context, so every enquiry starts blank.

## 4. Fourteen buildable ideas for a distinctive interaction model

The thread running through all of them: **a conversation in Pulse always starts from a stated purpose, a shared context and a recorded consent, and it always ends.**

1. **Open Doors (spontaneous, consented introductions).** A person or business opens a door for 24 hours or a week, tagged with what they are open to (suppliers, a cofounder, a cleaner, a coffee with a local baker). Others may knock with one context card; the owner sees a capped number of knocks and accepts or lets them lapse, and only on acceptance do contact details unlock. It beats connection requests and cold DMs because the invitation is explicit and temporary. Risk: it can be used for harassment or dating; mitigate with purpose tags, knock caps and easy blocking.
2. **Enquiry Cards that carry their context.** An enquiry is generated from the exact listing, role or service and pre-fills what is known (item, quantity, suburb, date needed, budget range), so the seller never answers "is it available" again. It beats the blank text box on Marketplace and Gumtree. Risk: extra fields reduce volume and feel like forms; keep to four fields and allow skipping with a stated reason.
3. **Needs and Offers board with intent matching.** Users post short, expiring needs ("need a plumber Thursday in Soweto") and offers ("two spare seats in my delivery van to Durban"), plus role intent and availability windows; matching is computed from those, not from follower counts. It beats a feed because relevance comes from declared intent. Risk: thin early supply; start in one city and a few categories, and auto-expire stale posts so the board stays honest.
4. **The Quiet Door for passive career discovery.** A professional marks themselves privately "open to being found" for chosen skills, salary band and areas, without a CV published. Employers post hiring signals (team, skills needed soon) before a vacancy exists; they see only counts and anonymous match strength ("6 people match") until a professional taps yes to reveal themselves. It beats LinkedIn's "Open to Work" banner and Indeed's resume database, which expose people to their current employer and recruiter spam. Risk: small cohorts can re-identify people; require a minimum group size (for example five) before showing any count and never reveal to the person's current employer.
5. **Quote Slips.** A request goes to at most three matched providers, each replying on a standard slip (price or range, earliest date, what is included, what is not). Providers pay nothing to see the request; any monetisation is on completion. It beats Bark and Thumbtack lead-selling by capping competitors, standardising comparison and removing the pay-before-you-know loss. Risk: a price-only race can emerge; show scope and response reliability next to price, and revenue needs another source.
6. **A Verification Ladder with honest labels.** Replace one tick with named checks and dates: phone confirmed by one-time code, business registration checked with CIPC, address confirmed, work shown by completed jobs. Unchecked claims are labelled "claimed". For informal traders, add non-document rungs such as vouching by past customers. It beats a blue tick that no one can interpret. Risk: a wrong check creates liability and false confidence; show scope and expiry, and never imply safety beyond the check.
7. **"Why you're seeing this" on every recommendation.** Each suggestion lists the actual inputs (you asked for X, you are in Y, this business offers Z) with a control to remove one input. It beats opaque ranking on LinkedIn, Instagram and Pinterest. Risk: explaining ranking aids manipulation; explain the user-facing reasons, not the scoring weights.
8. **A feed that ends: the Daily Round.** A fixed batch of items (for example 12), ordered by relevance and deadline, closes with "You are caught up"; unseen items carry over only if still relevant, and the user chooses daily or weekly. It beats infinite scroll because users can finish, which builds trust. Risk: lower time on site clashes with ad-driven revenue, which is a deliberate choice for Pulse to avoid ads.
9. **Knock budgets for anti-spam.** Everyone gets a small weekly allowance of cold contacts that refills when people reply and shrinks when messages are reported; first messages cannot carry links; new accounts start with a smaller budget; allowances cannot be bought. It beats paid InMail and open DMs because spam becomes costly without paywalling genuine users. Risk: new honest users start constrained; provide a verified-business route and an appeal.
10. **A consent ledger (POPIA-native).** Every permission is a stored record with who, purpose, scope and expiry, visible in plain language ("Moyo Plumbing may message you about your quote until 30 Nov"), with one-tap withdrawal and objection. It beats buried settings and satisfies the Act's purpose-limitation logic by construction. Risk: the ledger is itself personal data; keep it minimal and give users an export and delete path.
11. **Earned reviews.** A review is possible only after a completed thread (enquiry, quote, done) that both sides confirm, shown with job category and value band, with simultaneous reveal so neither side can retaliate. It beats Fiverr and Google-style open reviewing where purchased or pressured reviews circulate. Risk: off-platform deals leave no reviews; allow a lighter "I worked with them" confirmation by both parties.
12. **Close-the-loop rules.** Every enquiry, application and knock must end as answered, declined with a reason, or expired; response and closure rates appear on profiles; employers who post a hiring signal must update its status within a set window. It beats the silence that ghost jobs and ghosted enquiries create. Risk: canned decline messages; keep the reason list short and honest, and treat closed-without-reply as the failure it is.
13. **A WhatsApp and SMS bridge.** Counterparties without an account can answer an enquiry by replying on WhatsApp or opening a magic link, so a township trader never needs to learn a new app. It beats expecting adoption of yet another network when about 74 percent of surveyed SA township SMEs already use WhatsApp. Risk: dependence on Meta's templates, costs and opt-in rules; design the SMS and email path as the fallback.
14. **Safe Handover for local trades.** For goods and in-person services, the flow reminds buyers and sellers to confirm payment inside their own banking app (not a screenshot), suggests public handover points, and lets a user share the arrangement with one trusted contact. It beats leaving users to guess when fake proof of payment and robbery-lured meetings are documented in SA. Risk: it cannot guarantee safety; word it as guidance and never as protection.

## 5. What a web app on shared PHP hosting can and cannot do well

I did not inspect your actual host, so treat this as typical cPanel and CloudLinux behaviour. The Dot memory notes also record that all apps share one crontab on that host, so any scheduled work must be written with locking.

| Capability | Works well | Weak or not viable |
|---|---|---|
| Matching | Rule-based scoring in SQL over declared fields (category, suburb, availability window, skill tags, expiry), precomputed into a candidates table by cron every few minutes; fully explainable, which fits idea 7 | Large-scale machine-learned ranking, vector similarity over millions of items, heavy per-request joins |
| Search | MySQL FULLTEXT or SQLite FTS with proper indexes, filters by suburb and category, stored synonyms; fine into the low hundreds of thousands of records | Typo-tolerant, multilingual, relevance-tuned search at scale (Elasticsearch or Meilisearch need a VPS or managed service) |
| Notifications | Email via authenticated SMTP with SPF, DKIM and DMARC; Web Push through a PHP library and service worker; WhatsApp or SMS via provider HTTP APIs and webhooks; digests built by cron | High-volume bulk mail (shared hosts throttle outbound mail per hour; use a transactional provider); instant fan-out to many users |
| Real time | Short polling or Livewire-style polling every 10 to 30 seconds, "new message" badges, Server-Sent Events with care | Persistent WebSockets or many open connections: PHP workers and CloudLinux limits on entry processes cap concurrency, and long-lived processes are usually killed; a hosted real-time service (Pusher-style or Ably) is the realistic escape |
| Queues and jobs | Cron-driven queue runner that drains and exits; scheduled digests and expiry sweeps | Always-on workers, supervised daemons |
| Files and media | Moderate image uploads with resizing; direct-to-storage uploads for larger media | Video hosting or transcoding |

Design consequence: choose purposeful, bounded and slow-paced interactions (a daily round, expiring doors, quote slips) rather than live-feed behaviours. This is a fit rather than a compromise, because the model in section 4 is asynchronous by intent. Keep a clean seam so search or real time can move to an external service later without rewriting the product.

## 6. POPIA and abuse considerations that should shape the design

**POPIA principles to build in.**
- **Consent and lawful basis.** Processing needs a justification such as consent, contract or legitimate interest; make the basis explicit per purpose. Consent must be specific and withdrawable, which supports the consent ledger (idea 10).
- **Minimisation and purpose limitation.** Collect only what each feature needs (the Quiet Door should not hold a full CV) and do not reuse data for a new purpose without a fresh basis. Set retention periods and delete expired doors, needs and quotes.
- **Section 69 on unsolicited electronic direct marketing.** Opt-in is the default; the Information Regulator's 2024 enforcement notice against FT Rams Consulting treated post-hoc opt-out as no cure. Commentary describes a once-only consent request and a narrower existing-customer route with an opt-out in each message; I could not confirm the exact wording of the once-only limit, so check the Act and the Regulator's December 2024 direct-marketing guidance note. A 2026 commentary also describes a separate National Consumer Commission opt-out registry under the Consumer Protection Act, which would be an extra check on top of POPIA. Practical rule: Pulse should never let one user's business message another user marketing content without that person having opted in to that sender or category, and should keep platform notifications strictly transactional.
- **Right to object and automated decisions.** Provide objection and withdrawal in one tap. Section 71 limits decisions made solely by automated processing, so matching must not auto-reject anyone in hiring without human review.
- **Security and breach duties.** Hash credentials, encrypt sensitive fields, log access, and have a breach-notification procedure.

**Abuse threats and design answers.**

| Threat | Evidence in the market | Design answer |
|---|---|---|
| Spam and cold outreach | LinkedIn InMail and recruiter spam; Marketplace time-wasters | Knock budgets, context cards, no links in first messages, reply-rate visibility |
| Impersonation | Fake recruiters on LinkedIn; fake shops using real business details on TikTok Shop; fake learnerships misusing real institutions' names in SA | Verification ladder with named checks, re-verification on changes to contact details, report-and-freeze path |
| Scraping | Public profiles are the raw material for spam and scams | No public bulk-listable profiles by default, rate limits, no exposure of phone or email until consent, honey-token entries to detect scrapers |
| Fake reviews | Fiverr purchase rings and pressured reviews | Earned reviews tied to confirmed threads, simultaneous reveal, anomaly checks on review rings |
| Scam payments and fake proof of payment | Gumtree and Marketplace SA warnings | Handover guidance, warnings on off-platform payment requests, flags on pressure language |
| Discrimination in hiring | South African law (the Employment Equity Act) covers direct and indirect discrimination, and commentators warn that proxies such as postcode can stand in for race; no local case on automated tools found | No matching on special personal information or protected-ground proxies, human review of any automated shortlist, explainable matching, audit logs, anonymous-until-yes for first contact |
| Minors | POPIA sections 34 and 35 generally bar processing a child's data unless an exception such as parental consent applies | Age gate at 18, no profiles for under-18s, no targeting; if youth work-seeking is ever added, require a verified guardian-consent route and take legal advice first |
| Physical safety | SA police reports of robberies at arranged meet-ups | Public meeting suggestions, share-with-contact option, no automatic display of home addresses |
| Review or complaint retaliation and doxxing | Glassdoor's 2024 incident showed users react strongly to forced real names | Pseudonymous public reviews with verified private identity, clear disclosure of who can see what, no identity changes without the user's action |

**Net recommendation for the design brief.** Pulse should differentiate on consent, context and closure rather than on feeds, scale or cheap matching: every contact is permissioned, every enquiry carries its reason, every thread ends, and nothing about a person is shown until they say yes. That is buildable on shared PHP hosting with SQL matching, cron digests and polling, and it directly addresses the ten frustrations above.
