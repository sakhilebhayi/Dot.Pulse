# Dot.Pulse: the next generation of business social interaction (R-41)

Owner brief, 2026-11-08, every requirement kept, wording tightened. Working order: build and design everything in the brief, continue until done, ask for no permission, stop only after the deployment at the end. Awwwards skill only for design and frontend; Laravel Boost only for backend; say so if a tool is missing.

## 1. Vision
Dot.Pulse is where business happens naturally: consumers and sellers, employees and employers, businesses and customers, entrepreneurs and opportunities, professionals and organisations, people with ideas and people who can make them happen. Not LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, a marketplace or a job portal with another logo. Connections should feel spontaneous, effortless and enjoyable. Fun, confident, bold, premium, energetic and slightly bossy, with the credibility of a serious business platform. Ambition: people proud to say "I'm on InfoDot".

## 2. Philosophy (five principles)
1 Discovery without friction. 2 Spontaneous connections. 3 Personality, not generic corporate software. 4 Exclusivity through quality, credibility and useful access, not artificial barriers or empty status symbols. 5 Real world outcomes: a sale, a relationship, a job, a partnership. Every feature must serve this.

## 3. Tools
Awwwards skill (design, typography, motion, interaction, hierarchy, responsive; avoid generic SaaS dashboards and recycled social layouts). Laravel Boost (inspect, architect, implement, optimise; follow the project's conventions; real backend, not demos; preserve working functionality).

## 4. Audit first
Architecture, database, authentication and InfoDot integration, user and business profiles, existing social and discovery, messaging and notifications, feeds, media, search and recommendations, permissions, API integrations, UI components, broken or superficial features. Classify every feature: fully functional, needs improvement, partial, UI only or placeholder, broken, missing. Do not assume a feature works because its interface exists.

## 5. Market
LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Pinterest, X, Facebook Marketplace, Indeed, Glassdoor, directories and marketplaces: discovery, feeds, profiles, recommendations, messaging, commerce, recruitment, community. Find where they are repetitive, transactional, overwhelming or disconnected. Do not copy; establish a new interaction model.

## 6. The experience
An active business environment, not a conventional social network. Discover, Meet, Connect, Explore, Engage, Trade, Work, Collaborate as natural parts of the experience. Discovery surfaces businesses to buy from, products and services, employers, jobs, complementary professionals, new businesses, partnerships, local opportunities and events, discussions and useful knowledge; dynamic and personal, balancing relevance, freshness, diversity and unexpected discovery, not an endless chronological feed. Spontaneous interaction: a consumer meets a seller with what they need, a seller meets interested people, an employer meets matching skills, a professional meets an aligned company, two businesses find a partnership, an owner meets someone with a solution. Contextual introductions, discovery moments, low friction ways to start conversations. Not everything depends on connection requests: express interest, start a conversation, ask a question, request a quotation, explore an opportunity in context. No intrusive random matching, forced interaction or unwanted messages; users control how and when others approach them.

## 7. Consumers and sellers
Sellers showcase products, services, offers, new arrivals, updates, announcements, customer information, contact and enquiry options. Consumers discover, explore profiles, ask questions, express purchase interest, request quotations, compare offerings, contact, save and share. Contextual commerce from discovery to enquiry with no unnecessary steps. Not a copy of Dot.Emall: discovery and relationships, with handoffs to commerce where available.

## 8. Employees and employers
Employers: showcase the organisation, culture and environment, share opportunities, highlight needed skills, discover relevant professionals, start appropriate conversations, invite candidates. Professionals: present identity, skills, experience, achievements, discover organisations and opportunities, express interest, start conversations, control discoverability. Passive career discovery: meet an employer before a vacancy exists. Complement Dot.HR; where real integrations exist move from discovery into Dot.HR workflows.

## 9. Identity and profiles
Types: individual, professional, business, employer, seller, each presenting suitable information. Business: what it does and offers, products and services, location and operating areas, credibility and verification, updates, contact and enquiry options. Professional: skills, experience, interests, achievements, career interests, availability and discoverability preferences. Distinctive, explorable, credible. Integrate InfoDot business verification where the real API exists; clearly separate verified from unverified claims.

## 10. Personality
Confident, playful, ambitious, stylish, energetic, premium, professional, a little cheeky when appropriate. Concise memorable microcopy, not corporate language; not childish, arrogant or unprofessional. Memorable interaction details, delightful transitions. No unnecessary gamification, points, badges or leaderboards.

## 11. InfoDot membership
Genuine value: a useful business ecosystem, quality identities, discovery, credible information, relevant connections, better ways to find customers and opportunities, seamless access to other Dot platforms. Tasteful membership identity. No artificial scarcity, misleading status labels, fabricated verification or manipulative tactics.

## 12. Feed and content
Smarter, purposeful feed. Content types: business announcements, product and service showcases, employment opportunities, professional achievements, business discussions, questions and answers, partnerships and collaborations, events and invitations, industry updates, community discoveries. Presentation fits purpose (a showcase is not a text post, a job is not an announcement). Encourage discovery and meaningful interaction; do not reward spam, outrage or empty engagement. User control over feed, interests, visibility and notifications.

## 13. Messaging
Direct messages, business enquiries, product and service questions, employment conversations, collaboration discussions, conversation requests, attachments where supported, contextual references (the product or the opportunity that brought the parties together). Use existing Dot messaging where available. Privacy controls, blocking, reporting, spam prevention, abuse handling.

## 14. Local and global discovery
Nearby businesses, local jobs, regional networks, national markets, international discovery; by location, industry, interests, skills, category. No precise location without consent; no fabricated location data.

## 15. Personalisation and AI
Understand interests and intentions; improve discovery; find opportunities; recommend connections and conversations; help businesses improve profiles; help sellers present offerings; help professionals communicate skills; detect spam, impersonation, suspicious behaviour. Recommendations must be explainable. Protect privacy and respect permissions. Use Dot.Agents, Dot.Brain, Dot.Memory only where real capabilities and APIs exist. No simulated AI, no claimed integrations that do not exist.

## 16. Notifications
Genuine opportunities and useful updates: interest from a customer, an employer discovering your profile, a business response, a matching opportunity, a collaboration invitation, news from a business you follow. No spam or artificial urgency. Controls for frequency, categories and channels. Dot.Notify if its integration supports it.

## 17. Ecosystem
Real integration opportunities: InfoDot (identity, business profiles, verification), Dot.Emall, Dot.HR, Dot.Engage, Dot.Mines, Dot.Analytics, Dot.Files, Dot.Notify, Dot.Agents, Dot.Brain, Dot.Memory. Actual APIs, authentication, permission models. No simulation, no dead end links. InfoDot stays the only identity gateway.

## 18. UI/UX
Instantly understandable, memorable, smooth, fun without distraction, premium without intimidation, professional without boring, consistent across desktop, tablet and mobile. A layout that does not resemble a social feed. Navigation, discovery surfaces, profiles, showcases, composition, contextual controls, search, messaging, responsive, empty, loading and error states, accessibility, motion. Avoid generic dashboards, excessive cards, unnecessary gradients, oversized navigation, repetitive rounded containers, generic AI illustrations, decorative animation that slows the experience, clutter, fake activity indicators, controls that look interactive and do nothing. Its own visual language.

## 19. Backend and security
Identity and business relationships, profile types and permissions, content and feed architecture, discovery and recommendation services, messaging relationships, enquiries and opportunity workflows, media storage, search and filtering, notifications, moderation and reporting, audit trails, background jobs, caching and performance, API security, rate limiting, tenant and business data isolation. Server side authorisation; protect private profiles, conversations and business information; protect against spam, scraping and automated abuse; validate content and uploads; strict tenant separation where applicable; secure integrations; privacy and POPIA controls; record administrative and security events.

## 20. Retention
Personalised discovery, relevant updates, saved opportunities, interesting new businesses, relevant jobs, new connections, meaningful conversations, a personalised daily discovery summary, a clear sense of what is happening in the user's business environment. Measure meaningful outcomes, not screen time; every visit valuable, no endless scrolling.

## 21. Measure what matters
Active consumers, sellers, employers and professionals; profile engagement; product and service enquiries; consumer to seller and employer to professional connections; job engagement; conversation initiation and response rates; successful enquiries; discovery to conversion; repeat meaningful interactions; spam and abuse rates; retention and satisfaction; referrals into the InfoDot ecosystem. Real outcomes only; no fabricated metrics; never present an interaction as a successful transaction unless it was.

## 22. Roadmap
Phase 1 audit and discovery. 2 experience and design (identity, navigation, discovery, interaction model, responsive core). 3 core connections (consumer to seller, employer to professional, profiles and showcases, contextual enquiries and conversations). 4 intelligence and personalisation (relevance, explainable recommendations, Dot intelligence, notifications). 5 ecosystem integration. 6 testing and production readiness (end to end, desktop, tablet, mobile, performance, security and privacy review, defects, operational documentation).

## 23. Acceptance
1 A consumer discovers a real business and starts an enquiry. 2 A seller publishes and manages real offerings. 3 An employer presents an organisation and discovers professionals. 4 A professional presents skills and discovers employers. 5 Contextual conversations. 6 Control over profile visibility and discovery. 7 Verification status is accurate. 8 Discovery is based on real data. 9 Notification control. 10 Block and report. 11 Search, discovery and feed work end to end. 12 InfoDot authentication works. 13 Integrations use real APIs. 14 Private data stays protected. 15 Mobile and desktop fully usable. 16 No fake activity, fake metrics or non functional controls. 17 Important workflows pass automated and manual tests. 18 Acceptable performance under realistic use. 19 Graceful errors. 20 Meaningful journeys without administrator intervention.

## 24. Definition of done and final report
A distinctive coherent identity; an intuitive enjoyable discovery experience; real consumer to seller and employer to professional interaction; functional business and professional profiles; reliable conversations and enquiries; meaningful personalisation; secure backend; genuine InfoDot integration; responsive accessible design; verified end to end workflows; production ready error handling and monitoring. Final report: what existed, what changed, what was built, what was repaired, which integrations are operational, what remains, tests and results, security and performance findings, remaining production blockers. No claim of completion for anything not implemented and tested.

Standard: not another social network; the place where business meets opportunity. "Dot.Pulse: where business happens naturally."
